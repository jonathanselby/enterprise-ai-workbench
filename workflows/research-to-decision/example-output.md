# Research to Decision

## Question
Should Bafflewick replace the warehouse scanners this year or extend the current fleet for 18 months?

## What the evidence supports
The current fleet has a material reliability problem, with 14% failing at least once per shift. A limited pilot indicates the proposed replacement can improve pick-confirmation speed and can integrate with the existing warehouse API. Funding is available this financial year.

## What the evidence does not support
The evidence does not establish performance in the freezer warehouse, long-term reliability of the new devices, or completion of cyber approval. It also does not establish that the incumbent battery supply will actually remain available for three years.

## Contradictions
The incumbent describes battery availability as a three-year position but supplied no contractual commitment. That should not be treated as equivalent to assured supply.

## Unknowns
- Freezer-warehouse performance.
- Cyber approval of the Android device management profile.
- Whole-of-life support cost of the proposed devices.
- Whether the current-year budget can be carried forward.

## Options
1. Replace the fleet this year after completing the two remaining gating checks: cyber review and freezer pilot.
2. Extend the current fleet for 18 months and accept continuing reliability risk.
3. Replace non-freezer devices this year and defer freezer devices pending testing.

## Decision criteria
Operational reliability, cyber acceptability, cold-environment suitability, funding availability and transition risk.

## Recommended decision
Proceed toward replacement this year, conditional on successful cyber review and a short freezer-warehouse validation. Do not commit solely on the existing pilot.

## What would change the decision
A failed freezer test, unacceptable cyber findings, or evidence that the replacement cannot be funded without displacing higher-priority work would justify extension or phased replacement.
